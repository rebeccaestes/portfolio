Case Studies

Avoq

Showing the full range of a firm's work

Problem: Avoq's new CEO, comparing the site with peer agencies, felt it didn't show the full range of what the firm does: strategic communications, government relations and advertising.

Approach: Working from our in-house designers' designs, I built the About page, the Careers page and a dedicated page for each of the firm's three practice areas: Strategic Communications, Government Relations and Advertising. I built the pages in Gutenberg, so the marketing team can add and update pages with blocks, and I configured scroll animations to match the new design.

Outcome: The new pages launched in September 2026, giving each practice area its own page that the marketing team can update without developer help.

Built with: WordPress, Gutenberg, ACF, PHP, JavaScript, CSS/Sass

Fulbright Program

Giving editors control of their own pages

Problem: Fulbright's editors already used blocks in posts, but the rest of the site still ran on fixed ACF templates. Any layout change on those pages meant a request to me, so the developer became a bottleneck for routine updates.

Approach: At the client's request, I moved the remaining pages to the Gutenberg block editor. I turned the existing ACF templates into custom blocks with ACF fields, so editors kept the familiar fields but could arrange them freely, while preset brand colors, fonts and spacing kept every page on-brand. I also built a private reference library, visible only to logged-in users, that showed every custom block in use, and set up redirects alongside WordPress's automatic ones so existing links kept working.

Outcome: Editors could change page layouts themselves instead of asking a developer for each change.

Built with: WordPress, Gutenberg, ACF, PHP, Sass

National Native American Veterans Memorial

A website for a national design competition

Problem: Congress commissioned the National Museum of the American Indian to build a memorial to Native American veterans on the National Mall, and the design was chosen through an international competition. The museum needed a public website to launch the competition, guide entrants to a vendor-run submission system, and later present the finalists and the winning design.

Approach: I built the competition site in WordPress and worked with the museum's submission vendor on how the site connected to their system. I was responsible for every update to the site through 2019, adding pages as the competition moved from open call to finalists to the jury's decision, including the finalists' designs, the jurors, the advisory committee and the winning design.

Outcome: The competition drew more than 120 proposals from around the world, and the jury selected Harvey Pratt's Warriors' Circle of Honor in June 2018.

Built with: WordPress, HTML, Sass, JavaScript
